## 1. Frozen claims

### 1.1 Original target

Let (G=(V,E)) be a finite simple graph, (n=|V|), and let (d_k(G)) count the (k)-subsets (S\subseteq V) for which (G[S]) has an edge. The proposed theorem is

\[
 k(n-k)d_k(G)^2\ge (k+1)(n-k+1)d_{k-1}(G)d_{k+1}(G)
 \qquad(1\le k\le n-1).
\]

Equivalently, (d_k(G)/\binom nk) is log-concave. This is Conjecture 4.1 in the latest inspected version of Xie and Xu [XX]. The source continues to state it as a conjecture in its arXiv revision of 10 October 2026 and its October 2026 journal publication. No later resolution was found in the searches recorded here.

### 1.2 Stronger proposed result after simplification

Allow singleton witnesses, interpreted as graph loops. For nonnegative activities (w_v), define

\[
 d_k(G,w)=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq V,\ |S|=k\\S\text{ contains an edge or loop}}}
                  \prod_{v\in S}w_v.
\]

The proposed weighted theorem is the same ULC inequality with (d_k(G,w)), with order (n) equal to the whole ground-set size. Zero coefficients at the ends, and the identically zero case, are handled explicitly in the proof rather than by changing the order.

The stronger combinatorial claim is that

\[
 a_k(G)=\#\{S\subseteq V:|S|=k,\ S\text{ and }V\setminus S\text{ both contain a witness}\}
\]

satisfies

\[
 \frac{a_0(G)}{\binom n0}\le\cdots\le
 \frac{a_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(G)}{\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}},
 \qquad a_k(G)=a_{n-k}(G).
\]

This is antipodal pairs unimodality (APU) for the uniform measure on the dependent-set family. All nonzero conditional measures are of the same kind, or are full product measures. The resulting CAPU property implies ULC by **existing** Kahn–Neiman theory [KN]. Positive external fields cancel in antipodal products:

\[
 \left(\prod_{v\in S}w_v\right)
 \left(\prod_{v\notin S}w_v\right)=\prod_{v\in V}w_v.
\]

Thus the weighted conclusion is a consequence of the new proposed APU/CAPU input to an established implication; the implication and the external-field cancellation are not claimed as new theorems.

### 1.3 Exclusions and distinctions

- Ordinary log-concavity of (d_k(G)) is Horrocks's 2002 theorem [H02].
- Raw symmetry and unimodality of (a_k(G)) are Horrocks's 2000 theorem [H00]. The proposed strengthening divides by (\binom nk); raw unimodality alone does not give this.
- The ULC special cases and the classification of real-rooted dependence polynomials in [XX] are existing results.
- The conjecture's statement is already public. The contribution under consideration is its general proof and the stronger theorem, not the invention of the statement.
- The graph theorem does not settle the separate conjectures on more general independence systems in [XX], including its current Conjecture 4.4.
- ULC for all positive vertex activities is not being conflated with real-rootedness, the Rayleigh property, complete log-concavity of the full multivariate polynomial, or coefficientwise positivity of a symbolic inequality.
- Computation is corroboration only. No finite set of graph tests proves the universal theorem.

Taking complements reverses the rank sequence, and ULC of order (n) is invariant under this reversal because (\binom nk=\binom n{n-k}). For positive weights, complementation also inverts the activities and introduces the common factor (\prod_vw_v). These are elementary identifications, not new mathematical bridges.

The signed clique polynomial of a trace monoid is also sometimes called a “dependence polynomial.” It is a different object and was excluded from apparent title matches.

### 3.2 Generating functions and invariants

For (m) disjoint edges,

\[
 D_{mK_2}(x)=(1+x)^{2m}-(1+2x)^m,
 \qquad
 d_k=\binom{2m}k-2^k\binom mk.
\]

The doubled-dependent polynomial in this family is

\[
 A_{mK_2}(x)=(1+x)^{2m}-(1+2x)^m
             -x^m(x+2)^m+(2x)^m.
\]

For a graph with (n) vertices and an unlooped vertex (v), the independent-set recurrence is

\[
 I_G(x)=I_{G-v}(x)+xI_{G-N[v]}(x).
\]

The canonical dependence polynomial is the difference from ((1+x)^n); there is no universal graph-independent rational denominator to fingerprint. The displayed graph-family polynomials are already factored normal forms. Adding an isolated vertex multiplies (D_G) by (1+x). For a loopless graph, (d_0=d_1=0), (d_2=|E|), and (d_n=1) when there is an edge. These identities prevent a change of object or normalisation from masquerading as a proof.

No spectral matrix is part of the eventual proof, so the matrix and kernel fingerprint stage is inapplicable to this contribution.

### 3.3 Fingerprint of the simplified proof

The proof reduces to a set-family lemma. If a downset (\mathcal J\subseteq2^U) has no two members whose union is (U), if (A\subseteq U), and if (a\le b), (a+b=|U|), compare

\[
 \#\{J\in\mathcal J:|J|=a,\ J\cap A\ne\varnothing\}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \#\{J\in\mathcal J:|J|=b,\ J\cap A\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

Partitioning by the first marked element reduces the exceptional class to Katona's (t)-intersecting shadow bound. In the complementary-rank case the binomial factor in that bound is exactly one. This is why searches were rerun using union families, downsets, intersecting shadows, and antipodal terminology.

For graph-dependent sets, if (c(S)) counts vertices whose deletion makes (S) independent and (C_k) is its sum over doubly-dependent (k)-sets, incidence counting gives

\[
 (k+1)a_{k+1}-(n-k)a_k=C_{k+1}-C_{n-k}.
\]

The source of novelty, if the complete proof is validated, is this route to the **normalised** inequality and its graph application. Katona's shadow theorem and Kahn–Neiman's conversion are established inputs.

### [F16] Frankl, explicit formulation of Katona's inequality

Peter Frankl, *Linear independence, a unifying approach to shadow theorems*, European Journal of Combinatorics **57** (2016), 36–39. DOI: [10.1016/j.ejc.2016.04.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2016.04.001). [Author PDF](https://www.renyi.hu/~pfrankl/2016-3.pdf); [repository metadata](https://real.mtak.hu/44215/).

**Inspection:** full author PDF and publisher/repository bibliographic metadata. The theorem in its introduction states that for a (t)-intersecting family (\mathcal F) of (r)-sets,

\[
 |\Delta_s(\mathcal F)|\ge |\mathcal F|

 \qquad r-t\le s\le r.
\]

At (s=r-t) the ratio is one. This exactly validates the cited shadow step; that step is a use of a classical theorem, not a new shadow theorem.
